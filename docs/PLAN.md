# Physical Hydrology — plan of the book

Graduate online textbook for the Physical Hydrology course (Department of Civil Engineering, JBNU), built as a Jupyter Book with the same setup as Hydraulics and Engineering Hydrology.

- **Coverage baseline:** Brutsaert, W. (2023), *Hydrology: An Introduction*, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press. Every topic in its 14 chapters is covered here, except frequency analysis and the historical afterword, which are covered by the companion books.
- **Gap fillers:** Dingman (2015), *Physical Hydrology*, 3rd ed. adds the global and climate context, the critical zone and the soil-water chapters.
- **Research layer:** the instructor's research thread (complementary relationship, Budyko, Kirchner's storage–discharge model, stress tests) runs through Parts II and IV.
- **Licensing:** reference books are used only to fix the scope. Titles, ordering, text, derivations, figures, tables, examples and problems are all new (Section 6).

---

## 1. Design principles

1. **Audience.** First-year graduate students who have had undergraduate hydrology (the Engineering Hydrology book) and fluid mechanics (the Hydraulics book). Undergraduate material is linked, not repeated: rational method, unit hydrograph basics, NRCS-CN and design storms.
2. **From conservation laws to catchments.** Each process chapter starts from mass, momentum or energy conservation, makes the simplifying assumptions explicit, and ends with the lumped form that is used at the catchment scale (a "parameterization"). This is the spine of Brutsaert's book, and here it is made the visible organizing idea.
3. **Theory → derivation → computation → data.** Same order as the undergraduate books, with one more step at graduate level:
   - each chapter ends with a **Colab lab** that applies the theory to data;
   - each chapter has a short **"Open questions"** section pointing to current research.
4. **Chapter template.** The undergraduate template plus three additions:
   - Core ideas;
   - sections, with derivations in full;
   - worked examples in Python;
   - **Lab** (new);
   - common mistakes;
   - problems with folded answers;
   - **Open questions** (new);
   - **Key papers** (new);
   - further reading.
5. **Conventions carried over.** American English, SI only, all figures generated by code, the same palette, chapter-tagged equations (n.k), numbers glued from code, and a "Lab homepage" header link.
6. **Companion books.**
   - Frequency analysis (Brutsaert Ch. 13): the Statistical Hydrology book.
   - History (Brutsaert Ch. 14, Dingman App. G): *A Brief History of Fluid Mechanics and Water Sciences*.
   - Open-channel hydraulics: the Hydraulics book.

## 2. Proposed table of contents (16 chapters + appendices)

Coverage keys: **B** = Brutsaert (2023) section, **D** = Dingman (2015) chapter. They fix the scope only; titles, order and all content here are original.

### Part I — Foundations

**1. Hydrology as a geophysical science**
Sections:
- Scope: the continental water cycle and the global balance
- Stores, fluxes and residence times
- Scales and the problem of parameterization
- Physical vs. systems approaches
- Field equations: material derivative, Reynolds transport, conservation of mass, momentum and energy
- Water balance of a control volume
- Measurement and uncertainty

Coverage:
- B §1.1–1.5
- D Ch. 1

**2. Energy, climate and the water cycle**
Sections:
- Radiation laws and the planetary energy budget
- Greenhouse effect
- Atmospheric composition
- General circulation and teleconnections (ENSO, monsoon)
- Global distributions of P, E and Q
- Aridity and the partition of precipitation (first look at Budyko)
- The critical zone and biomes
- Water-cycle intensification under warming (Clausius–Clapeyron)

Coverage:
- D Ch. 2
- B §1.3 (2nd-ed. climate-change additions)

### Part II — Water in the atmosphere and its exchange with the surface

**3. Water vapor and the lower atmosphere**
Sections:
- Humidity variables and thermodynamics of moist air
- Hydrostatics, lapse rates and static stability
- Potential temperature
- Turbulence and the Reynolds decomposition
- Turbulent fluxes and eddy covariance

Coverage: B §2.1–2.3; D Ch. 3

**4. The atmospheric boundary layer and surface fluxes**
Sections:
- Structure of the ABL
- The logarithmic profile and roughness
- Monin–Obukhov similarity and stability functions
- Bulk transfer and aerodynamic resistance
- The surface energy budget as a boundary condition, and the energy-balance closure problem
- Heterogeneous surfaces and internal boundary layers (blending height)

Coverage: B §2.4–2.7; D Ch. 3

**5. Precipitation**
Sections:
- Cloud microphysics and precipitation formation
- Weather systems, including atmospheric rivers and the East Asian summer monsoon
- Space–time structure of rainfall (areal estimation, radar–gauge merging, geostatistics)
- Measurement errors and their correction

Coverage: B §3.1–3.3, 3.6; D Ch. 4

**6. Interception and snow**
Sections:
- Canopy storage and interception models (Rutter-type and Gash-type)
- Snowpack physics: metamorphism, cold content, liquid water
- Snowpack energy balance and melt
- Temperature-index models and their energetic basis
- Snow in a warming climate

Coverage: B §3.4–3.5; D Ch. 5

**7. Evaporation: physical theory**
Sections:
- Molecular and turbulent transfer of vapor
- Mass-transfer formulations
- Energy-budget formulations and the Bowen ratio
- Combination equations: Penman, Penman–Monteith, big-leaf vs. two-source
- Equilibrium evaporation and Priestley–Taylor
- Transpiration and stomatal control
- Open water and lakes

Coverage:
- B §4.1–4.3
- D Ch. 6

**8. Actual evaporation at landscape scale**
Sections:
- Potential vs. actual evaporation
- The complementary relationship (Bouchet, advection–aridity, the generalized CR)
- Water-budget estimates: catchment, soil, lake
- Long-term partition: Schreiber–Ol'dekop, Budyko, Fu and steady-state forms
- Evaporation climatology and trends (the evaporation paradox)
- Remote sensing and reanalysis products

Coverage:
- B §4.3.3, 4.4, 4.5 (2nd-ed. additions)

### Part III — Water on and below the land surface

**9. Shallow-water flow over land and in channels**
Sections:
- Depth-averaged mass and momentum equations (Saint-Venant)
- Friction laws (Manning, Chézy, Darcy–Weisbach, laminar sheet flow)
- Characteristics, wave celerity and the hierarchy of approximations (dynamic, diffusion, kinematic)
- Froude and kinematic-wave numbers
- Roll waves and other features

Coverage:
- B §5.1–5.4
- Links to the Hydraulics book

**10. Overland flow and flood-wave routing**
Sections:
- Overland flow on a plane: kinematic solution, rising hydrograph, equilibrium time
- Lumped overland-flow models
- Flood waves in rivers: kinematic and diffusive limits
- Muskingum and Muskingum–Cunge, derived from the diffusion wave
- Parameter estimation

Coverage: B §6.1–6.3 and §7.1–7.3

**11. Physics of porous media**
Sections:
- Porous materials and their description (texture, structure, pore-size distribution)
- Capillarity and the retention curve (Brooks–Corey, van Genuchten)
- Hysteresis
- Darcy and Buckingham–Darcy
- Conductivity functions
- Field equations: Richards and saturated flow
- Diffusivity form and similarity
- Measurement

Coverage:
- B §8.1–8.4
- D Ch. 7

**12. Infiltration and exchange at the soil surface**
Sections:
- Phenomenology of infiltration
- Sorption: Boltzmann transform and sorptivity
- Philip's series, Green–Ampt, Parlange-type solutions
- Infiltration under rainfall and ponding time
- Catchment-scale losses and spatial variability
- Capillary rise, bare-soil evaporation and the stages of soil drying

Coverage: B §9.1–9.6; D Ch. 8

**13. Groundwater outflow and baseflow**
Sections:
- Unconfined riparian aquifers and the Dupuit–Forchheimer approximation
- The Boussinesq equation and its exact and linearized solutions
- Sloping aquifers and the kinematic-wave limit
- Recession analysis: −dQ/dt vs. Q (Brutsaert–Nieber), early and late time
- Baseflow decline rates and groundwater storage change (GRACE)
- Regional groundwater in the hydrologic cycle

Coverage:
- B §10.1–10.6 (2nd-ed. additions in §10.6)
- D Ch. 9

### Part IV — Catchment response to precipitation

**14. Streamflow generation**
Sections:
- Mechanisms: infiltration excess, saturation excess and return flow, subsurface stormflow, groundwater ridging, macropores
- Riparian zones and headwaters
- Old and new water: tracers and hydrograph separation
- Variable source areas and topographic indices (TOPMODEL)
- Summary of mechanisms and parameterization options

Coverage:
- B §11.1–11.3 (2nd-ed. old/new-water simulation)
- D Ch. 10

**15. The catchment as a dynamical system**
Sections:
- Linear stationary response: impulse response, unit hydrograph theory, Nash and other conceptual responses
- Identification of response functions: moments, deconvolution, regularization
- Nonlinear lumped response: storage–discharge relations, Kirchner's sensitivity function, doing hydrology backward
- Nonstationary response
- Annual runoff from annual precipitation

Coverage:
- B §12.1–12.5, Appendix A.2–A.3

**16. Models, uncertainty and change**
Sections:
- Purposes and types of hydrologic models
- Calibration, equifinality and evaluation (NSE, KGE, split-sample)
- Model structure uncertainty
- Bottom-up stress tests and scenario-neutral assessment
- Land-use and climate change detection

Coverage:
- D App. F
- Instructor's research (scenario-neutral flood risk, CR in ESMs)

### Appendices

- **A. Mathematical tools.** Leibniz rule; similarity solutions; linear systems (convolution, Laplace and Fourier); responses of nonlinear systems; numerical methods for PDEs. Coverage: B App. A, extended.
- **B. Properties of water and moist air.** Coverage: D App. B.
- **C. Units, dimensions and conversion of inhomogeneous equations.** Coverage: D App. A.
- **D. Solar geometry and clear-sky radiation.** Coverage: D App. D.
- **E. Field methods.** Stream gauging, eddy covariance, soil moisture, tracers. Coverage: D App. E plus Ch. 3.
- **F. Data sources.** Reanalysis (ERA5), GRACE, MODIS, national networks. Includes how to access them in Colab.

**Not covered here:**
- Frequency analysis (B Ch. 13): Statistical Hydrology book.
- History (B Ch. 14): water-history book.

## 5. Figures, examples and labs to generate

- **Figures.** Every figure is drawn by code:
  - schematics as matplotlib patches (cycle, ABL structure, soil profiles, hillslope mechanisms);
  - physics plots from equations (profiles, retention curves, Boussinesq solutions, recession plots, CR curves, Budyko curves);
  - synthetic-data plots (turbulence time series for eddy covariance, rainfall fields, hydrographs).
- **Examples.** Numerical examples are invented or synthetic, as in the undergraduate book, unless real data are allowed (open question 2).
- **Labs.** Colab notebooks in `labs/`, linked from each chapter. Candidates:
  - eddy-covariance flux computation (synthetic 10 Hz data);
  - MOST profile fitting;
  - CR and Budyko with ERA5;
  - Boussinesq recession;
  - Kirchner tank;
  - unit-hydrograph identification;
  - a TOPMODEL index from a synthetic DEM.

## 6. Licensing rules

- Reference books (Brutsaert 2023; Dingman 2015) fix the scope only. Chapter and section titles, their order, text, derivation write-ups, figures, tables, worked examples and problems are written from scratch.
- Standard equations may be stated; derivations and explanations are our own.
- Figures from books or papers are never reused; they are redrawn from code. Published numbers are cited from their primary source, in our own table layout.
- Real data are used only in the labs, with source and license stated.

## 7. Decisions (2026-10-19)

- Title: Physical Hydrology. 16 chapters + 6 appendices, as above.
- Real data: labs only; worked examples and chapter figures use synthetic data.
- Frequency analysis and history are left to the companion books.
